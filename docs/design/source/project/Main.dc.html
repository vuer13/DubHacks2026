TrailSense design system

Listen first. Read at arm's length.

TrailSense is a mobile web app for blind and low-vision hikers. The phone hangs on the chest or sits in one hand while a vision model speaks short hazard warnings and a voice agent answers questions. The hiker mostly listens, so every screen is built for VoiceOver first, then for a sighted teammate glancing at it in direct sunlight. Dark theme only. Two accents, forest green and fork blue, plus a three-step severity scale and one SOS pair. Nothing else.

3. Components

Every control is a real button or switch with an aria-label, at least 64px on its shortest side, and carries an icon or word alongside any colour it uses.

Mic button

Idle surface-2 fill, accent-bright ring
Listening accent fill, one slow outer pulse
Agent speaking inverted, waveform glyph, no motion

112px on the hiker screen, 96px minimum. Centred at the bottom of the screen where a thumb lands without looking.

4. Spacing and touch targets

A 4px base. Gutters are 16px at every screen edge. No tappable control is smaller than 64px on its shortest side, with at least 12px between neighbours so a gloved thumb cannot hit two.

5. Accessibility rules

Every control is a native button, switch or link with an aria-label that says what it does and, for toggles, its current state. The last spoken line lives in a single region marked aria-live assertive and aria-atomic, so a new warning interrupts whatever VoiceOver was reading; everything else on the screen is polite or silent. Colour is never the only signal: each severity carries an icon and its name in words, each health dot sits beside a status sentence, and the fork blue always travels with the fork glyph. Focus rings are a 3px off-white outline with 3px offset, visible on every fill in this theme including the SOS yellow. Motion is limited to one slow pulse on the listening mic and the easing of the countdown ring, and both stop under prefers-reduced-motion. Nothing on the hiker screen is set below 18px, nothing tappable is under 64px, and all type scales with the user's system text size.
